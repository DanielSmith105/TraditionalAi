	    Application or Bust?

The modern application paradigm is seldom app-
reciated beyond infinity displays and the high-
priced designer fonts contrasting the desktop
windows applications and unmanageable registry keys
from which it came. For those who remember the early
Windows 3.1 applications that were slow enough in
loading that you could see the brute force instant-
iations happening as the foreground took focus 
over the minimized state. In fact - the
entire GUI (graphical user interface) eventually
became synonymous with application windows as the
reason for turning on the computer. Because fam-
ously, Bill Gates didn't know what to do with more
than 68 KB (kilobytes). 

People eventually figured out what to do, building
apps such as Shazam or Winamp or Spotify and those
who stuck with the typewriters were eventually left
behind by spell check and Britney Spears Youtube 
videos in the background instead of living room
records. The Windows operating system (OS) - as separate 
from the Windows applications running on them pioneered
an entirely new design of HCI (human computer interface)
centred on the GUI and a file system par-
adigm. It dealt with its fair share of abuse from
early authors of code, programming all sorts of 'warez'
and 'trojans' into various executables. As the name
suggests, 'executables' are application instantiations
built by 3rd party authors and programmers specifically
for executing windows subroutines into user applications.
The applications are dependent on memory, compiled GPU 
runtime threads and HCI user inputs to make it go. As 
a result of trust in its registry and device driver
cybersecurity constraints, all kinds of professionals 
gained
certificates and diplomas towards becoming ever
more experienced in Microsoft infrastructures, esp-
ecially as it relates to enterprise solutions with 
platforms like .NET and SharePoint. Other OEMs (original equipment manufacturer)
followed and opened up their products for these new digital markets,
allowing people all over the world to place their own stick-
ers and widgets onto operating systems and application GUIs. The
net became full of 'skin', 'plug'ins' and 'add-ons' for the few
who dared putting their credit cards onto some random server's
formulaire, though admittedly not unlike some mail-order T-Shirt from the Eaton's 
catalog of 1966. The net generally and Microsoft specifically became bloated 
with spyware, randsomware and other battle scars of professionalism
in-waiting, and not until the so-called "Paypal Mafia" featuring
Elon Musk (Space-X), Peter Thiel (Y-Combinator) and others who led 
the charge with online properties E-Bay, Paypal and LinkedIn and Yelp
brought exactly the right fuel necessary to create approximately 300 million jobs
in the new 'gig' economy, estimates the World Bank.

Having done away with executable installs, Apple next revolu-
tionized
the world by retailing an 'App Store' market. A secure
totalitarian online ecosystem of Nerds, monitoring controls on any 
and all applications loaded atop their formidable opera-
ting systems built on notebooks, desktops and mobile
phones. Applications had gone from complicated IT executables
requiring varying messes of competing software drivers,
sometimes with sticker prices in the hundreds of thousands
for enterprise customers of Oracle or VMWare to a well-
managed, low-overhead market and payment portal. Apple and new 3rd
party Dev teams began charging $9.99 for Angry Birds and 'add-on' avatar skins 
for shiny new customers. Increasingly relying on DevOps, Testers 
and Architects all working together in new employment conditions,
free cafeteria food, co-working spaces, designing in Waterfall, 
Agile or Skunkworks methodologies, within modern sandboxed development rounds
and internal hackathons until 3am or whenever the rotten eggs
were gone.

All this came crashing down on Apple shortly after it had 
beaten out Adobe Flash as the gateway to internet media. Subsequent to
successfully lobbying out from the woodworks HTML5 and ceas-
ing its support for any Flash add-ons from its browsers also
made Apple less vulnerable and solidified its quality operating
system started in the late 1970s, even further. The FBI then 
decided that it should have unfettered 
access to
Apple iPhone backdoors for "investigative purposes" and in gathe-
ring evidence against criminals so-called. It would have been 
akin to warranting seizure of your photo albums, rolodex, 
mail deliveries and even the kitchen sink if the 
FBI was suspicious enough about your person to merit such an
occupation of your time, during routine traffic stops as with  
gangster drug busts. The moment this happened all applic-
ations loaded 
atop your smartphone would be vulnerable to these technolog-
ical
backdoors the FBI was demanding Apple build for them. The
public reaction was as expected: Apple shouldn't be forced
to do anything it deems unsafe on its devices, even if law
enforcement believes it should have priority over the life-
blood of modern devices inside digital ecosystems and online
communities. This probably speaks volumes for the mentality
of law enforcement over the past few centuries. Mainly we
should trust law enforcement exactly because they have access
to the backdoors of a city, snuffing out crimes and opport-
unities from the nooks and cranies of jewellery stores
and butcher shops alike. No one doubts that law enforcement prob-
ably does have priviledged access to what is seen in a muni-
cipality nor that it should have but somehow the logic becomes
absurd once that access involves our personal cell phones as
glorified family albums whereby it could be revealed that
one time, instead of searching for 'deviled eggs' the 
you typoed yourself into 'defiled eggo' websites with unspeakable
amounts of butter and burned toast.

Apple still hasn't fully recovered from that mess and while
applications are nevertheless thriving in communities as Youtube
Spotify and Netflix can attest to (accounting for its attempted 
takeover of major Hollywood studio WB). But security and data have
not been fully sorted and all kinds of people everywhere are at 
risk of Ai conversations leaking their therapy-like data to any-
one with the checkbook to pay for it. Just like the FBI back-
door which was itself rumoured to have been built anyway by
some activist hackers for a few thousand dollars. It's unclear
whether applications will remain front and center within new
paradigms of machine learning modeling and Ai vibe-coding but one point which
remains emphatic is that IT systems are now shared in expert-
ise by OEMs (original equipment manufacturers) like Apple, the 
authors who manage the codebases and increasingly average cust-
omers
who can build their own agentic architectures to repair the under-
lying frictions between hardware sensors and our true intent-
ions. These new software resiliences and sustainabilities 
will 
increasingly be with those who access to the code, open, closed 
or provided with keys and secret handshakes to engineers qualif-
ied to read increasingly anti-fragile systems.
