🔬 Windows Outlook Ai

Windows Outlook :

There aren't many Microsoft Windows applications which are
more pervasive than Outlook. In the early days of email
while Hotmail was competing with AOL for online supremacy
Outlook was the default (offline storage) solution for many
Windows users. Cloud computing has changed all that.

The modern Outlook application can now be run in the cloud
as part of Microsoft's 365 offering as well as a desktop
application which inexplicably still requires an internet
connection just to run. It would be unfair to accuse Google and all its
Chromium devops to have converted open source browser
behaviours - akin to protection rackets on offline files,
while secretly creating data-stream applications that simply
can't be refused. Youtube, Spotify and Netflix are all ex-
amples of these new temptations, for $10 a month and the
irresistible buffet of Ai-curated materials. There may
be no turning back. I can't help but hesitate when keeping
files on my laptop in case for example it gets dammaged
in transit from IP address to IP address. I love to work
at coffee shops and airports alike, adding mobility rights
to the modern technical professional office experience.

Let me list a few significant behavioural differences be-
tween online and offline activity, especially relevant as
we come to depend more and more on Ai models for our careers,
cultures and assets. After all, Kanban boards can only go so
far towards keeping you sane within developer tickets.

Online:
- Data exists atop external server computers (cloud).
- Data is usually owned by the cloud possessor/ owner.
- Ai/ agentic workflows can be applied instantly, think 'spell check'
for curating your many lives of business.
- Security is nearly flawless, incorporating infinite entropy &redundancies
of backups, fail-safe, disaster-recovery, etc. But searching it
yourself might just be a nightmare.

Offline:
- Data exists atop your physical hard-drive or memory device.
- Data is often packaged in DRM security, giving the makers
some semblance of control and ownership or even copyright at all.
- Security is only as good as your physical means of protection
and the password sheets you accidentally threw out last week.


I grew up alongside the laptop. Me and this one other kid at school
were the only ones sporting them, even among an entrance
class of software engineers and computer scientists. Yep - I'm
kind of that old. Before Chromebooks and other thin client
operating systems, many laptops were competing in marketing
categories such as ultrabooks, gaming, etc. Google was secretly
building all these web properties such as Maps, Gmail, Docs
and had convinced themselves of fighting with anything res-
embling a desktop (offline) operating system. "Don't be evil" slowly
morphed into "The web is so great". And so it is with
great sadness that I write today about the online requirements
from Outlook. The other hidden subtext that I haven't touched upon
yet is now the unmistakable fingerprint of Ai within the devil
and the details. And this matters a great deal for our freedoms
of expression and other secret police censorships that now go
by the name of 'shadow bans' or 'doxing'. Phishing used to be a
simple email from an obviously Nigerian prince. It's gotten so
elaborate that I'm not entirely sure whether I am in the wrong line
of work. Even own own ability to search within our inbox, sort junk
mail or receive messages from competing interests, have all probably been filtered
and checked by an incoming spam-trained Ai model. Much of this infliction of Ai
apon our (forced) online activities and behaviours leads to an
inversion of our initial social contract with these tools. Email, as a res-
source for connecting people inside of technology instead of
paying a price for postage. Postage is still great for all kinds of
things but email no longer required us to pay a cost, attached to it
other than an ISP provider.

"13624 Microsoft.Windows.Internal.AI.ContentModeration.winmd"

So when I came across this file on Windows upon walking through
my file system, buried deeply inside - I was obviously concerned
with what might be happening to all of my messages. I'm not
insisting that Email should instead be treated as some Burqa
of technological means to shroud one's files in secrecy, though
these applications do exist as full-duplex channel encryptions but -
surely there is a balance to be achieved between new data
rights, personal privacy and the level of control desired by
capitalist institutions ruling their techno-fiefdoms with as
much totalitarianism as can be informally consented to for
$9.95 a month and a side order of fries.

Just a reminder that Ai is a powerful resource but also one
that requires us paying attention and with this new sight into
the edges of our computations we can also demand more acc-
ountability. After all, we are paying them for exactly
that reason.

The file structure of this mysterious file is very typical of standard Windows
structures and is not altogether out of place among
drivers and system commands within 'system32' or registry entries - but
further investigations might reveal how this particular
file is nested inside other PIDs or executables. It did not show
up in any processes allocated RAM but might also be buried
inside the olk.exe (Outlook) runtime. In any event - it's certainly
a new Windows addition and is likely a sign of its mental-
ity going forward with Copilot in the cloud and on the desk-
top. No one from Microsoft has volunteered an opinion on this
or any of their online/ offline preferences but they sure are
pushing their 365 business lines rather hard.
