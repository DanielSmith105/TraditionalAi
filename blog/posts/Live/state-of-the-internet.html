State of the Internet

An important aspect to remember about the internet while exuberances
of application and mobile supercomputers dominate our thoughts is that
this smitany has only been upon us for a few decades now. We have more
shared history with caves in France than we do these new silicon virtual
realities. That isn't to say that we should aspire to spend more time in
caves - just that we shouldn't so easily dismiss these natural histories
and instead think strongly about how we choose to incorporate these new technologies
into our lives. When Newton first published the Principia in 1687, a work of
art as much as science, well-known at the time but was not
formally accepted by the Church until some 200 years later. Newton's ideas and
philosophies became popular but in absolute numbers not even as widespread
as today's small pond influencers amassing thousands of likes on platforms that
have un-inundated cobblestone villages and into tech hubs and online
megacities a-like. The pace of innovation has truly hit its redline at
somewhere around 5,550 revolutions per minute.

blished standards made by tech-oligarchs running these companies. And upon failing to "cross the
chasm" the regular public is stuck somewhere in between a user and a human, unsure which
takes precedent. In China, land of centralized economic controls, one smartphone
application rules them all, acting as a credit score, community payment portal and
social media gateway, as a reflection of society for language-agnostic tourists,
much like ancient waterways do fan out into thousands of streams and lakes.
